# The Complete Guide to Managing Guest RSVPs for Weddings and Events

> **In short:** "How many people are coming?" is a question that follows anyone planning a wedding or party right up to the last night. The good news: managing guest attendance no longer takes a notebook, a pen and a hundred phone calls. In this guide we explain what RSVP means, why it matters for your budget, and how to manage it step by step with minimal effort.

---

## What does RSVP mean?

**RSVP** stands for the French phrase *Répondez s'il vous plaît*, meaning **"please reply"**. Simply put, the host is asking the guest to **confirm whether they'll attend** before a given date.

It may look like a small detail, but it's actually **the foundation of planning any event**.

---

## Why does confirming attendance matter so much?

### 💰 Because it controls your budget

Most venues and caterers charge **per person**. Every wrong estimate has a price:

- **Estimate too high:** you pay for plates and seats that stay empty.
- **Estimate too low:** guests without seats, and an embarrassment nobody wants on a day like this.

### 🪑 Because it's the basis of your seating plan

You can't arrange tables, or decide who sits next to whom, until you know who's actually coming.

### 🧘 Because it calms your nerves

Couples who know their exact numbers two weeks before the wedding live the final days far more calmly.

---

## The classic problems with traditional RSVPs

If you've organized an event before, these will sound familiar:

- Guests who **never reply**, despite reminders
- A guest who says "I'm coming" and then shows up **with four people**
- Replies scattered across **WhatsApp, phone calls and messages passed on by relatives**
- A spreadsheet that changes every day, with nobody sure which version is the latest
- **Relatives abroad** who haven't said whether they'll travel for the wedding

The solution isn't more effort; it's **a smarter system**.

## Step by step: managing RSVPs like a pro

### 1. Set an RSVP deadline

Put a clear deadline in the invitation, usually **two to three weeks before the event**. That gives you enough time to give the venue your final number and arrange the tables.

### 2. Ask clearly for the name and number of guests

"Yes, I'll come" isn't enough. Ask every guest for:

- **Their name**
- **How many people** will come with them

And set **a maximum number of plus-ones** so you're not surprised by numbers you didn't plan for.

### 3. Use a personal link for each family

Instead of one general link, you can create **a personal link for each family or guest**. Each reply is then matched to them automatically in your guest list, without anyone having to type their name and without mix-ups between similar names.

### 4. Make replying as easy as possible

Every extra step means fewer replies. The ideal reply takes **two taps on a phone**, with no app, no sign-up and no password. At **eInvite.me**, guests confirm right inside the invitation, and you receive the reply instantly.

### 5. Give guests who can't come a kind way to take part

A guest who can't attend still wants to share your joy. At eInvite.me they can leave you **a voice message** with their reply, so a polite "no" becomes a lovely keepsake.

### 6. Follow everything from one dashboard

Instead of collecting replies from everywhere, they all appear in **one dashboard**:

- Who has **confirmed** and how many are coming with them
- Who has **declined**
- Who has **opened the invitation** but not replied yet
- Who **hasn't opened it** at all

That last distinction is gold: a guest who hasn't opened the invitation needs a reminder message, while someone who opened it without replying may need a friendly call.

### 7. Send a gentle reminder before the deadline

A few days before the deadline, send a short reminder **only to those who haven't replied**. For example:

> "Hi 🌸 We're looking forward to your reply to Sami and Rita's wedding invitation before 1 June, so we can arrange everything perfectly. Here's the link 👇"

### 8. On the day: QR check-in

Guest management doesn't end with the reply. At eInvite.me, every confirmed guest receives **a QR code** that's scanned at the door, so you know who has actually arrived and keep the entrance flowing without long paper lists.

## Extra tips from experience

- **Make clear who the invitation is for**, e.g. "invitation for two" or "adults only", kindly and without awkwardness.
- **Don't expect 100% of replies:** some guests may never answer. Allow a small margin and call the important ones.
- **Keep one source of truth:** make the invitation the only reference for times and places, so conflicting information doesn't spread.
- **Put one person in charge** of following up on replies, to avoid duplication and confusion.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can online RSVPs be used for events other than weddings?**
Of course: birthdays, engagements, baptisms, baby showers, and private or corporate events.

**What if a guest changes their mind after replying?**
They just need to let you know, and you update their status in your guest list. That's exactly why a realistic deadline helps: the closer it is to reality, the fewer last-minute changes.

**What if a guest loses their QR code?**
If they have a personal link, they just open the invitation again and their code appears.

**Can guests see each other's replies?**
No. Replies are visible only to you, in your dashboard.
