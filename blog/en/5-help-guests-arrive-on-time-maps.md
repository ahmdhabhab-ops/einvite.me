# How to Help Guests Arrive on Time and Find Your Venue with Digital Maps

> **In short:** A guest lost on the road, calls asking "where's the venue?" minutes before the bride walks in, a ceremony starting in a half-empty room... These moments are easy to avoid. The secret is clear information that reaches guests where they need it: on their phone. Here's a practical guide to making your guests' arrival smooth and on time.

---

## Why are guests late in the first place?

Before looking for solutions, let's understand the real causes:

- 📍 **An unclear address:** "after the bridge, on the right, near the gas station..."
- 🕐 **Ambiguous timing:** is the time on the invitation when to arrive, or when the ceremony starts?
- 🚗 **Traffic nobody planned for:** in many cities, this can't be ignored.
- 🅿️ **Looking for parking** takes longer than anyone expects.
- ⛪ **More than one venue on the same day:** a church or mosque first, then a reception somewhere else.
- 📄 **The card is at home:** the guest is in the car, and the address is on a paper card left on the table!

---

## 1. Make the location a button, not a long description

The most important step of all: **don't just write the address**. Add **a directions button** that opens the venue straight in the guest's maps app.

At **eInvite.me**, every venue in the invitation has a **"Get directions"** button that opens it on Google Maps with one tap. Guests go from the invitation to navigation in one move, with no copying, pasting or searching.

> 💡 **Tip:** Write the full, exact address as it appears on Google Maps (the venue's name and area), then test the button yourself to make sure it opens the right place and not somewhere with a similar name.

---

## 2. More than one venue? Separate them clearly

Many weddings take place in two venues or more:

- **The religious ceremony:** a church, a mosque, or wherever the marriage contract is signed
- **The reception or dinner:** in a hall, restaurant or garden

For each venue:
- ✅ **Its own address**
- ✅ **Its own directions button**
- ✅ **Its own time**

That way, no guest heads to the reception while the ceremony is at the church!

---

## 3. A clear day schedule prevents confusion

**The day's schedule (timeline)** is the best friend of punctuality. Instead of one time at the top of the invitation, show the full program:

| Time | Program |
|---|---|
| 4:00 PM | Ceremony |
| 5:30 PM | Reception and drinks |
| 7:00 PM | Dinner |
| 9:00 PM | Cake and dancing |

**Why it works:** guests know exactly **when to be where**, and plan their arrival accordingly.

> 💡 **Smart trick:** If the ceremony starts at 4:00, ask guests to **arrive by 3:30**. Half an hour of margin solves most lateness problems.

---

## 4. The countdown: a quiet, effective reminder

**A live countdown** in the invitation isn't just decoration: every time guests open it, they see how many days and hours are left. It fixes the date in their minds without a single annoying reminder message.

---

## 5. Small details that make a big difference

Add what guests actually need, on the venue page or in the invitation text:

- 🅿️ **Parking:** Is there a car park? Is there valet parking?
- 🚐 **Transport:** Is there a shuttle for guests from a set meeting point?
- 🏔️ **The road:** If the venue is in the mountains, mention winding roads or cold evenings.
- 👗 **What to wear:** If the party is outdoors, a reminder about heels on grass goes a long way!
- 🏨 **For guests from abroad:** suggest hotels near the venue.

---

This is where a digital invitation shines: **it's on the guest's phone**, in the same WhatsApp chat where it arrived. On the day, they don't need to hunt for a card or call anyone: they open the link, tap the directions button, and go.

Some guests won't make it however clear the directions are: family abroad, or someone held back at the last minute. **A live stream** inside the invitation lets them share the moment from anywhere in the world.
